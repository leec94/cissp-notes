## Exam Recap
- Exam taken July 30, 2026
- Written August 13, 2026

### Study Experience
For time, I spent roughly 4 months total. Studied leisurely (~0.5-1 hour per day) since April, reading chapters, listening to the audiobook of the Official Study Guide on Spotify, and watching LinkedIn Learning CISSP videos. Then registered at the end of May to take end of July, which was a huge motivator. From then I crammed those 2 months, 2-3 hours on weekdays and 6 hours on weekends, and aimed to take a practice exam each weekend.
My work study group gave me the advice to do between 1,000 - 5,000 questions to pass the exam, when I was about 1 month out. That was quite a shock, but ended up motivating me to immediately start taking practice questions when I was dragging my feet. This ended up being the best tip I got - I then changed my schedule to do ~50 questions on weekdays and ~100 questions or at least a practice exam each weekend. Going through all the questions really highlighted my weak points to know what to focus my time on.

### Exam Experience
The question difficulty felt manageable at the first third, then I could sense the difficulty slowly creep up as the questions went on. By question ~80 I could definitely tell the adaptive testing was throwing difficult questions at my weak domain, networking. For timing, I tried to take 1 hour for each 50 questions, and felt some stress when I was falling a bit behind, but overthinking the timing just made it harder to focus, so I tried not to think about it much. Nearing question 100 I was bracing to stay calm at each question beyond that, but at 100 the exam ended. It then gives you a survey, and you get your result as you were walking out the door. I got a fair amount of questions that I could guess the process it was asking about, and a good number were very similar to the practice questions and similar to the ones in the official study guide. Not much on AI though, surprisingly.

### Tips
- Know your processes and models: Incident Response steps, access control types, OSI model, etc. The official study guide is enough depth of what you need to know, but it’s important that you are well versed in the content it contains.
- Know your weaknesses. The exam is adaptive so it will ask you more and more difficult questions on your weakest domains. Spend extra time on your weak domains, but make sure to keep studying the other areas to maintain the recall on what you’ve learned.
- Simulate the exam environment. I did some practice exams in a quiet environment with minimal distractions, and occasionally even had a youtube video of a computer based exam room background sounds playing to get used to others typing in the background.
