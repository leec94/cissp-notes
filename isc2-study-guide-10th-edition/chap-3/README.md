# Chapter 3: Business Continuity Planning
## Notes
- Business continuity planning (BCP): assessing risks to organizational processes and creating policies, plans and procedures to minimize impact those risks might have on an organization if they were to occur
- BCP main elements
  - project scope and planning
    - organizational review: perform structured review of the business's organization from crisis pov
    - BCP team selection 
    - resource requirements
    - external dependencies
  - business impact analysis
  - continuity planning
  - plan approval and implementation
- SOC reports: maintained by AICPA to maintain consistency between auditors from different accounting firms
  - SOC 1: covers internal controls over financial reporting
  - SOC 2 or SOC 3: covers security, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy, or availability controls 
  - service-level agreements (SLAs): commitments to customers 
- business impact analysis (BIA) stages
  1. Identifying priorities
  2. risk identification
  3. likelihood assessment
  4. impact analysis
  5. resource prioritization 
- types of analysis
  - quantitative impact assessment: use of numbers and formulas to reach a decision, often in dollar value to businesses
  - qualitative impact assessment: non-numerical factors like reputation, investor/customer confidence, workforce stability, and other concerns into account. often results in categories of prioritization (high/medium/low)
- asset value (AV)
- maximum tolerable downtime (MTD) or maximum tolerable outage (MTO)
